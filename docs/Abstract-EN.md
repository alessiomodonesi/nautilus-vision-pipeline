# Integration, Optimization, and Benchmark of an Edge Vision Pipeline in ROS 2 for an Underwater Stereoscopic Watertight Module

This thesis is part of the Nautilus project at the University of Padua, aimed at developing platforms for the exploration and monitoring of aquatic environments and marine biodiversity. The work focuses on an underwater stereoscopic vision module housed within a watertight enclosure, designed to be mounted beneath a vessel's hull facing the seabed for optical sensing and submerged environment analysis.

Regarding physical integration, the project involved replicating and adapting the existing module inside a new, larger watertight enclosure through 3D modeling and additive manufacturing (3D printing) of a custom support to rigidly house both cameras and the embedded Raspberry Pi computing unit.

On the software side, the architecture was fully migrated and restructured within the ROS 2 Jazzy environment. Overcoming the limitations of the previous daemon-based management, a modular pipeline was developed comprising native drivers for synchronized acquisition, an underwater image enhancement node, and a stereoscopic target detection node with a standardized interface (vision_msgs). Time synchronization and distributed communication toward the main computational unit (NVIDIA Jetson) were established using Chrony and CycloneDDS.

The experimental contribution consists of profiling and benchmarking three operational scenarios (acquisition only, acquisition with detection, and acquisition with enhancement and detection). The edge inference models, optimized using OpenVINO, were evaluated by comparing accuracy (mAP), effective framerate, pipeline latencies (broken down into pre-processing, inference, and post-processing), and system resource utilization. Finally, an extended thermal stress testing campaign in a sealed enclosure characterized the onset of thermal throttling—induced by the lack of external air exchange and the limited thermal dissipation of the plastic housing—providing quantitative guidelines on the trade-off between local edge processing and computational offloading.
